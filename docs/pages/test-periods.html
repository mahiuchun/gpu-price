Which dates the results come from · 14 Nov 2026

Until today, every result came from one three-week window

I have now scored the forecasters on eleven separate weeks of our own prices, and on four years of AWS prices. Two results held everywhere. One was mixed. One did not hold.

What changed

Holds The engine beats drift by about 8% in all three tests: the earlier weeks, the test weeks, and four years of AWS prices.

Holds The five AI agents beat drift by 15% to 19% in all three. On our own prices they also beat the engine. On AWS prices they are level with it.

Mixed The ML model. On our prices its good result was two lucky weeks. On AWS prices it works well and beats the engine.

Does not hold Averaging the engine with the ML model on our prices. I recommended that file earlier and I withdraw that.

The periods

  • Before today: the engine, the ML model and every average were scored on weeks 9 to 11 only. The agents were scored on 7 days inside those weeks.
  • Added today: the same forecasters on weeks 1 to 8, and the agents on 7 more days in weeks 5 to 8.
  • Not extended: the AI scientist that tunes the engine. Its test needs the week-by-week setup and exists for weeks 9 to 11 only.

What to keep in mind

  • Weeks 1 to 8 were not hidden from me. I designed the engine while looking at data up to 16 Oct. Its settings were not tuned on those weeks, and it scores the same before and after, but only weeks 9 to 11 were blind for it.
  • The agents' model had never seen any of these dates. Its knowledge ends in April 2026. I wrote the agents' instructions once, after the test weeks had been looked at, and did not change them. The numbers on this page are for those first instructions.
  • The agents were scored on 14 days, not all 77. Most of those forecasts are 1 and 3 days ahead; only 61 are 7 days ahead.
  • Weeks 1 to 4 cover 17 prices, because one of the two main sites only started on 8 Sep.
  • AWS prices are a different kind of price. AWS sets them itself and they move in small steps. A result there says a method is sound; it does not promise the same on provider lists.
  • The agents' model may have met the AWS years in its training, since its knowledge runs to April 2026. It is unlikely to remember zone-level prices, but only one of the twelve AWS days is after that date.
  • One place the engine does not beat drift: the few of our prices that go back to January, mostly four large cloud spot lists. Over 1,757 forecasts from January to August it scores -1.8% (likely -6% to +3%), which is no different from drift.
