6 · WHAT HAPPENED

The engine works. Tuning it with an AI did not add to it.

  • The engine beat drift by about 8%. The true figure is somewhere between 2% and 14%.
  • The AI scientist made it slightly worse. Its ideas looked good on past weeks and did not hold up on new ones.
  • The shuffled copies agree. The engine beat drift there too, by 7% to 11%, so its edge comes from knowing the list. The AI's ideas changed nothing there.
  • The trained small model found one useful change (use 30 days of history, not 60). It is a little better than the engine alone, but not clearly.
7 · WHAT WORKED BETTER

Five AI agents that forecast each price directly

  • Each agent has a different job: read the list, watch the providers that reprice often, stick to how rarely prices move, weigh the other forecasts, look for jumps that may reverse.
  • Each one forecasts every price alone. Their answers are averaged.
  • They beat drift in three separate tests, including four years of AWS prices. Their forecast, after the coach's changes in the next section, is the one we hand in.
  • Letting them read each other's answers did not help.

The dates behind every number are on the test periods page.

8 · THE AGENTS IMPROVE

A coach AI changes the agents, and fresh days decide

  • Kept change 1: wider ranges for 3 and 7 days ahead. The agents had been too sure of themselves a week out. The truth landed inside their 7-day range 61% of the time; now 76%. The target is 80%.
  • Kept change 2: one agent rewritten. It now measures how often and how far this price moved before, and trusts a recent trend less the further ahead it looks.
  • The improved agents made about 4% less error than the first ones on the exam days. The true figure is somewhere between 0.5% and 7.6%.
  • A second check agreed: 3.4% less error on 14 other days (1,415 forecasts), somewhere between 1.6% and 5.1%.
  • Most of the gain is the wider ranges. The rewritten agent adds a little.
9 · A MODEL WE TRAINED

A small open model learned to forecast by practising

  • How it practised. The model read the same facts as the agents and gave a forecast. It got a point score for how much closer it was than drift. It did this 7,680 times on old AWS prices, and its weights were nudged toward the answers that scored well.
  • It then worked on our prices, a different kind of price it had never seen: from about 3% to about 10% less error than drift.
  • Repeating the training gave 5% to 10%, not a fixed 10%. We trained Qwen3-8B six more times on Modal GPUs. The exact repeat of the first run gave 10.1% again. Runs with a different random start gave 5% to 8%. Every run beat the untrained model.
  • The newer model, Qwen3.5-9B, needed a gentler training setting. With the setting our rule picked, two runs gave 6% and 3%, about where it started (5%). With the first run's setting, two more gave 9% and 10%. The two models end up about level.
  • Our rule picked the wrong setting for our prices. It chose the setting by watching AWS prices, and that setting was better on AWS and worse on ours.
  • At its best it is as good as our engine, and behind the GPT agents. Adding it to the agents as a sixth voice did not clearly help, so it is not in the forecast.
