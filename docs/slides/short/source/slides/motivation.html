Thin prices, and AI that can fool itself

GPU prices are thin

Compute is rented, leased and lent against like a commodity. But a published “GPU price” is the median of one site’s list of offers.

It moves when an offer joins or leaves, a provider reprices daily, or a euro price is converted each morning.

Needed: forecasts with honest ranges, days ahead, for lease rates, collateral and capacity budgets.

Self-improving AI is easy to fool

A loop that keeps whatever scores well on the data it tuned on will “improve” on noise.

The real question is which kinds of AI self-improvement still help on days the system has never seen.

Needed: a verifier the AI cannot change, and a final exam on unseen days.

RSI4GPR

[0:55–2:13] Two problems motivate this work. First, GPU prices are thin. Compute is now rented, leased and even lent against like a commodity. But a published GPU price is usually just the median of one website’s list of offers. It can move because an offer joins or leaves the list, because one provider reprices every day, or because a euro price is converted to dollars each morning. If you’re setting a lease rate or valuing collateral, you need a forecast with honest ranges. Second, self-improving AI is easy to fool. A loop that keeps whatever scores well on the data it tuned on will happily improve on noise. So the real question is not whether an AI can change its forecaster, but whether those changes still help on days it has never seen. That needs a verifier the AI cannot change, and a final exam on unseen data.
