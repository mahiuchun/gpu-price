The training pipeline. What is trained is the crowd’s configuration — its agents’ instructions and its range stretch — not model weights. Each round is propose, verify, keep. The current crowd forecasts every price on six practice days. A verifier scores those forecasts against drift and lists the worst misses. The coach AI, the generator, reads that and proposes one change. A second verifier re-scores the changed crowd: it must be better on the practice days and not worse on five hidden check days, where the coach only ever learns pass or fail. Only then does the change make crowd n+1, and the next round begins. The verifiers are fixed code that the AI cannot change, which is what makes the self-improvement trustworthy. Ten rounds: round 1 kept wider ranges, round 3 kept a rewritten base-rate keeper, round 8 passed practice but failed the hidden check, and the rest did not improve practice. A final verifier, the exam on five days of our prices the loop never saw, scored the final crowd 4% better than the first. That frozen crowd goes to the inference pipeline.
